# Single Social Posts, Ads & Profile Assets

> Purpose: The definitive guide to designing standalone social creatives — single-image posts, quote cards, announcements, story/reel covers, video thumbnails, profile assets, and paid ad visuals — that stop the scroll and read instantly on a phone.

**When to read this:** Designing any one-frame social graphic or ad — not a multi-slide swipe set. For multi-slide swipe carousels see [carousels.md](carousels.md). For full dimension tables and export settings see [../08-visual-composition/format-specs.md](../08-visual-composition/format-specs.md).

## The single-frame discipline

A standalone creative gets **one frame and ~0.4 seconds** in a fast-scrolling feed. There is no swipe to redeem a weak start, no second slide to deliver the payoff. So the entire job — stop, communicate, brand, drive action — happens in one composition. The constraint forces ruthlessness: **one focal point, one message, one action.** If you can't say what the single most important element is, the design isn't done.

Three things every single-frame creative must do, in order:
1. **Stop the thumb** — a strong focal point with contrast, motion-feel, faces, or pattern interrupt.
2. **Communicate one idea** — readable at thumbnail scale, without sound, without reading a paragraph.
3. **Brand + direct** — who it's from, and what to do next.

Defer the master list to [../08-visual-composition/format-specs.md](../08-visual-composition/format-specs.md); these are the few you'll reach for constantly.

| Creative | Aspect | Pixels |
|---|---|---|
| IG portrait post (max reach) | 4:5 | 1080 × 1350 |
| IG / general square post | 1:1 | 1080 × 1080 |
| Story / Reel / TikTok / vertical ad | 9:16 | 1080 × 1920 |
| LinkedIn / FB single-image feed ad | 1.91:1 | 1200 × 627 |
| YouTube thumbnail | 16:9 | 1280 × 720 |
| Profile avatar (display) | 1:1 circle-cropped | ≥ 400 × 400 |

Default to **4:5 portrait for organic feed reach**, **9:16 for stories/reels/vertical ads**, **1.91:1 for link-preview feed ads** on LinkedIn/Facebook.

## Single-image posts & quote cards

The workhorse format. Built on a single strong idea + a single visual anchor.

Composition:
- **One focal point.** A subject, a number, a short headline, or a single object. Use the rule-of-thirds / center per [../08-visual-composition/composition.md](../08-visual-composition/composition.md).
- **Generous margins.** Don't crowd the edges — keep key content within the centered safe rectangle (~middle 80%) so platform crops and UI don't clip it.
- **Type as image.** For quote/text cards, the text *is* the design: set it large (headline ~64–120 px on 1080), short measure (4–8 words/line), tight leading, one accent word emphasized. See [../02-foundations/typography.md](../02-foundations/typography.md).
- **Contrast or it disappears.** Light feed backgrounds eat light designs; add a defined background, border, or color block. 4.5:1 minimum on text.

> Do: One bold quote, 3 short lines, one word in the accent color, logo bottom-corner, lots of breathing room.
> Don't: A full paragraph shrunk to fit, centered over a busy photo with no scrim, logo competing with the message.

## Announcement & event graphics

Event, launch, sale, webinar, or "we're hiring" graphics carry **facts** (what / when / where / how) but must still read in a glance.

Hierarchy (most graphics fail by flattening this):
1. **The hook / what** — biggest. "We're live", "30% off", the talk title.
2. **The key detail** — date/time or offer, second-largest.
3. **The supporting facts** — venue, speakers, terms, smaller.
4. **Brand + CTA** — logo and "Register" / "Link in bio".

Group related facts (date+time together, place separate) using proximity and dividers, not a wall of equal-weight lines. One accent color to flag the single most actionable fact. Use 9:16 for story announcements; 4:5 or 1:1 for feed.

## Story & Reel cover design

Stories/reels are **full-screen 9:16 (1080 × 1920)** with heavy UI overlay. The cover frame (and any text-on-story) must dodge the chrome.

Safe zones (9:16):
- **Top ~250 px** — profile name, "Sponsored", progress bars. Keep text below it.
- **Bottom ~250–420 px** — caption, action rail, "Send message", CTA sticker, swipe-up/link area. Keep key text above it.
- **Right ~120 px** — like/comment/share/save rail on reels/TikTok. Keep faces/text left of it.
- **Safe core:** center the message in the **middle ~60%** vertically.

Reel **cover** specifics (the frame shown on the grid/profile): some surfaces crop the 9:16 reel cover to a **1:1 grid tile** — keep the cover's title text within a centered square so it survives both the full-screen reel view and the cropped grid thumbnail. Test the cover at grid-thumbnail size.

> Do: Title text in the middle band, faces left of the action rail, CTA sticker placed clear of the caption zone.
> Don't: Caption text at the very bottom (covered by UI), a face hidden behind the like button, title that gets sliced by the grid crop.

## Thumbnails (YouTube & video)

A thumbnail is judged at **tiny size** (as small as ~120 px wide in a sidebar) and against a wall of competitors. Bold or invisible — there is no middle.

The **3-element rule:** a great thumbnail has at most **three visual elements** — typically (1) a face/subject with clear emotion, (2) 2–4 huge words of text, (3) one supporting graphic or background. More than three and it's mud at small size.

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Big expressive face, eyes visible, strong emotion | Distant/small subject, neutral expression |
| 2–4 words, enormous, heavy weight, high contrast | A sentence; thin or small type |
| Bold color contrast, separation between subject and bg (outline/glow) | Subject blending into a busy background |
| Readable at 120 px — squint-test it | Detail only visible at full size |
| Consistent style across a channel (recognizable) | Every thumbnail a different look |

Design at 1280 × 720 but **always preview at thumbnail scale.** Use an outline/stroke or drop shadow to lift the subject and text off the background. Don't put critical content in the **bottom-right corner** — the timestamp overlay covers it.

## Profile assets

Small, persistent, brand-defining. Easy to neglect, high-leverage.

**Avatar (≥ 400 × 400, displayed tiny & circle-cropped):**
- Must read at **~32–48 px**. For a logo, use the **mark, not the full lockup** — wordmarks vanish. A single letter, icon, or a tightly-cropped face works.
- High contrast against both light and dark feed backgrounds; add a safe inner margin so the **circle crop** doesn't clip the mark.
- Consistent across every platform for recognizability.

**Banners / cover photos:** each platform has a different aspect *and* a different "safe" area because mobile crops differently from desktop and overlays the avatar/name.

| Asset | Upload size | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn personal banner | 1584 × 396 | Avatar overlaps lower-left; mobile crops sides |
| LinkedIn company banner | 1128 × 191 | Heavy mobile cropping — center key content |
| X / Twitter header | 1500 × 500 | Avatar overlaps lower-left; corners may round |
| Facebook cover | ~1640 × 856 (renders responsive) | Mobile shows a taller central crop |
| YouTube channel art | 2560 × 1440 | Only the center **1546 × 423 "safe area"** shows on all devices |

Keep logos, taglines, and any text inside each platform's **safe area**, clear of the avatar overlap and mobile side-crop. Verify with [../08-visual-composition/format-specs.md](../08-visual-composition/format-specs.md).

## Paid ad creative — anatomy

An ad must do the organic job plus survive an ad context (a "Sponsored" label, an explicit CTA button, often no sound, a skeptical viewer). Build to a clear anatomy.

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────┐
│  BRAND PRESENCE  ──────  logo/handle │  recognizable in <1s, not a watermark
│                                      │
│  VISUAL HOOK                         │  the scroll-stopper: face / motion /
│  ( focal subject, fills frame )      │  pattern-interrupt / bold contrast
│                                      │
│  ONE MESSAGE / VALUE PROP            │  single benefit, readable w/o sound
│  short, big, high-contrast           │  & w/o reading a paragraph
│                                      │
│  PROOF / CONTEXT (optional)          │  product in use, result, rating
│                                      │
│  CTA CUE  ───────────  "Shop now →"  │  reinforce the platform CTA button
└─────────────────────────────────────┘
   (platform overlays: Sponsored label, CTA button, caption below)
```

Principles:
- **Scroll-stopping hook in the first frame / first ~1 second.** For video, the visual hook is in second 1, not after a logo sting. For static, the focal point does it.
- **One clear message.** Ads with two value props convert worse than ads with one. Pick the strongest benefit.
- **Brand presence early and clear** — viewers should know who it's from without reading. But brand is a corner, not the hero.
- **Value prop visible without sound or reading** — assume muted autoplay and a 0.5s glance. Show the benefit visually (product in use, before/after, the result).
- **Reinforce, don't duplicate, the CTA button** — the platform supplies a button; your creative can point at it ("Tap to shop →") but shouldn't bury its own message under redundant chrome.

**The 20%-text guideline (history):** Facebook/Meta historically rejected or throttled ad images with **more than 20% of the area covered by text**. The hard rule was retired, but the *principle still holds* — text-heavy images perform worse and can be down-ranked. **Keep ad text minimal; let the visual carry it.** A 5×5 grid mental check: text shouldn't fill more than ~5 of the 25 cells.

## UGC vs polished creative

Two valid registers; match to placement and goal.

| | UGC / native style | Polished / branded |
|---|---|---|
| Looks like | A real person's phone video/photo | Designed, art-directed |
| Feels | Authentic, peer, "not an ad" | Premium, trustworthy, brand-led |
| Best for | Top-funnel, social proof, mobile-feed ads, reels/TikTok | Brand-building, premium products, hero campaigns |
| Risk | Can look cheap if too rough | Can be scrolled past as "obviously an ad" |
| Craft note | Looser framing, real lighting, captions, handheld | Tight grid, controlled light, brand system |

Even UGC needs craft: legible captions, a clear hook in the first second, and one message. "Native-looking" is a deliberate aesthetic, not an excuse for an unreadable frame.

## Static ad vs organic — the differences

| | Organic post | Paid ad |
|---|---|---|
| Audience | People who chose to follow you | Cold/strangers, skeptical |
| Context | No "Sponsored" tag, no CTA button | Ad label + platform CTA button |
| Job | Engage existing audience, build brand | Stop a stranger, drive one action |
| Brand | Assumed known | Must be established in the frame |
| Message | Can be subtle, in-jokey | Explicit value prop, fast |
| Text | More tolerated | Keep minimal (20% principle) |
| Variants | Usually one | Many, for A/B testing (vary one element at a time) |

Repurposing organic → paid: re-crop to the ad placement, strengthen the hook and value prop, add explicit CTA reinforcement, and pull back text density.

## Motion / video creative basics

Most feeds are video-first and **autoplay muted.** Design for sound-off, vertical, fast.

- **Hook in 1 second.** The first frame/second decides retention. Lead with the most striking visual or the payoff — not a logo intro. Make the **thumbnail/first frame** strong, since it's the still everyone sees first.
- **Captions / on-screen text always.** Most watch muted; the message must land silently. Burn in captions or large key-word text; don't rely on platform auto-caption alone for the hook.
- **Vertical-first (9:16, 1080 × 1920).** Shoot and frame for full-screen vertical; letterboxed landscape in a vertical feed wastes 60% of the screen and signals "repurposed".
- **Respect the same safe zones** as stories/reels (top ~250, bottom ~250–420, right rail ~120) — keep captions and key text out of the UI bands.
- **One idea, fast pacing.** Short clips, quick cuts on the hook, a single message, an explicit end CTA frame.
- **End card** with brand + CTA, held long enough to read (~1.5–2s).

For programmatic / templated video generation (data-driven captions, variant rendering, branded end-cards), use **Remotion** — see the pipeline, fonts, and render settings in [../13-production/production-and-tools.md](../13-production/production-and-tools.md).

## Platform fit — don't post one size everywhere

A single master size cropped to every platform produces clipped text and wasted space. **Adapt per placement.**

- **Reframe, don't just scale.** Moving 4:5 → 9:16 means re-composing — re-position the subject, re-flow text into the new safe zones, not stretching.
- **Respect each platform's safe zones and crop behavior** (feed vs story vs grid thumbnail).
- **Match the register** — LinkedIn skews polished/professional, TikTok/Reels skews native/UGC, Instagram feed sits between.
- **Export a size per placement** from one source design rather than one file everywhere. See [../08-visual-composition/format-specs.md](../08-visual-composition/format-specs.md) for the per-platform export matrix.

> Do: One concept, re-laid-out for 4:5 feed, 9:16 story, 1.91:1 ad — each respecting its own safe zones.
> Don't: Export a 1:1 and let every platform auto-crop it (heads chopped, text clipped, awkward bars).

## Consistency within a campaign set

A campaign is a *family* — varied but unmistakably related. Lock a small system and vary content within it (see [../08-visual-composition/brand-systems.md](../08-visual-composition/brand-systems.md)).

Lock across the set: type system, one accent palette, logo placement, layout grid, photographic/illustration style, and one recurring visual motif (a shape, a frame, a color block). Vary: the specific message, image, and per-placement crop. The test: lined up together, the set reads as one campaign; alone, each still works. For A/B ad variants, **change one variable at a time** (headline OR image OR CTA) so you learn what moved the metric.

## Accessibility

- **Contrast** 4.5:1 on text (3:1 large), measured against the actual pixels behind the text — add scrims/plates over photos. See [../02-foundations/color.md](../02-foundations/color.md).
- **Don't rely on color alone** to carry meaning — pair with icons, labels, or position.
- **Alt text** on every uploaded image; **captions** on every video.
- **Legible type** — no hairline weights at small sizes; avoid long all-caps strings; readable at thumbnail scale.

## Do / don't summary

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| One focal point, one message, one action per frame | Three competing ideas in one graphic |
| Design and test at thumbnail / muted / glance scale | Judge only at 100% on a big screen |
| Keep key content in the safe zone, clear of UI | Let captions/buttons cover your message |
| Big type, short lines, 4.5:1 contrast | Paragraphs of small low-contrast text |
| Brand visible fast, but as a corner not the hero | Logo so big it buries the message (or so small it's lost) |
| Re-compose per placement & export each size | One file auto-cropped onto every platform |
| Captions always on video; hook in second 1 | Rely on sound; bury the hook after an intro |
| Keep ad text minimal (≤ ~20% area) | Text-heavy ad image that gets down-ranked |
| Lock a campaign system, vary content | Each asset a totally different look |

## Agent checklist

- Decide the single focal point, single message, and single action before composing.
- Choose the size for the actual placement (4:5 feed, 9:16 story/reel/vertical ad, 1.91:1 link ad, 1280×720 thumbnail) — don't reuse one file everywhere.
- Test every creative at thumbnail / muted / 0.5-second glance scale, not just at full size.
- Keep all key text and faces inside the safe zone, clear of top/bottom/right UI bands.
- Set type big with short lines and 4.5:1 contrast; add scrims under text on photos.
- For thumbnails, use the 3-element rule: one expressive subject, 2–4 huge words, one graphic.
- For avatars, use the mark not the lockup; ensure it reads at ~32–48 px and survives the circle crop.
- For ads, hook in second 1, show the value prop without sound or reading, keep brand a corner, keep text ≤ ~20% area.
- Put video captions on always and frame vertical-first; make the first frame a strong thumbnail.
- Keep a campaign set consistent (locked type, accent, logo placement, motif); for A/B variants change one variable at a time.
- Add alt text to images and captions to video; never rely on color alone for meaning.
- Generate statics as HTML→PNG and motion via Remotion — see the production file.
